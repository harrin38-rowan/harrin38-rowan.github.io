df3 <- data.frame(
temp = c(79, 74, 73, 76, 82, 72, 71, 79, 83, 70, 73, 75, 77),
chirps = c(44, 40, 38, 43, 48, 37, 34, 45, 51, 32, 35, 42, 43)
)
lm(temp ~ chirps, data = df3)

Call:
lm(formula = temp ~ chirps, data = df3)

Coefficients:
(Intercept)       chirps  
     46.474        0.714  
rho = cor(df3$temp, df3$chirps)
mu1 = mean(df3$temp)
sd1 = sd(df3$temp) 
mu2 = mean(df3$chirps)  
sd2 = sd(df3$chirps)

beta1 = rho*(sd1/sd2)

alpha = mu1 - beta1*mu2

By multiplying the correlation of temp and chirps by the standard deviation of temp, and then dividing by the standard deviation for chirps, we get beta, the coefficient for the line of best fit. Then, by multiplying this beta with the mean of chirps and subtracting that from the mean of temp, we get alpha, the y-intercept for the line of best fit.
